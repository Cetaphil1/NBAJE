03 A Case in Point

A student likes engineering.
The major still grinds them down.

Liking the subject and surviving the day-to-day are two different problems. Here's how the gap shows up.

Interest profile

What pulled them in

Likes math Likes building things Likes technology Likes problem-solving Strong in AP physics "Engineering sounds right"
Quiz verdict → Engineering, high match

Real environment

What they actually meet

60-hour weeks Weed-out calc & physics Problem-set marathons Cold, competitive culture Abstract theory before practice "Not a real engineer" doubt Burnout by sophomore year Quietly considers switching
Real outcome → Likes engineering. Switches to applied math.

High interest doesn't guarantee sustainable fit. The environment has to match the student — not just the subject.

05 The Instrument

Eleven constructs. One question:
can you actually thrive here?

The Fit Beyond Interest survey measures eleven dimensions of person–major fit, drawn from established psychology and education research and adapted for undergraduate students. Each construct uses validated Likert items from the v2.1 instrument.

01 / InterestSurface

Interest in the subject

Whether the subject itself genuinely pulls you in — what you found stimulating before college and what you still find engaging now.

6 itemsAgree · 1–5
02 / EnvironmentContext

Environment fit

Whether your major's culture rewards how you naturally think — what's valued, what's rewarded, and whether your strengths land.

5 itemsAgree · 1–5
03 / BehaviorAction

Behavioral engagement

How often you actually engage with the field outside of class — reading, building, talking about it, going deeper voluntarily.

7 itemsFrequency · 1–5
04 / MotivationDrive

Motivation quality

What's actually driving you — genuine curiosity, or external pressure and habit. Quality of motivation predicts long-term persistence.

3 itemsAgree · 1–5
05 / BurnoutRisk

Burnout exhaustion

How drained the workload leaves you over a semester — emotional exhaustion, stress impact on performance, and questioning whether to continue.

3 itemsAgree · 1–5
06 / ResilienceBuffer

Resilience

Whether you stay motivated and engaged when work gets hard — and whether you can hold a healthy balance through demanding stretches.

3 itemsAgree · 1–5
07 / SatisfactionAffect

Major satisfaction

Your emotional read on the day-to-day experience — whether coursework feels rewarding and whether you'd choose it again today.

3 itemsAgree · 1–5
08 / PersistenceOutcome

Persistence & switch risk

Whether you'd still choose this major today, and how often stress or dissatisfaction has you reconsidering. The primary outcome the model is trying to predict.

2 itemsMixed · 1–5
09 / BelongingSocial

Belonging

Feeling at home among other students in the field — one of the strongest single predictors of staying in any major.

1 itemAgree · 1–5
10 / AdvisingSupport

Academic advising

How much real guidance you've gotten from advisors when making major-related decisions. Optional — with a "haven't met yet" branch.

1 itemAgree · 1–5 + N/A
11 / Global FitCriterion

Global fit rating

A single overall rating of how well your major fits your interests, strengths, and long-term goals. The criterion variable for validating the deeper model.

1 itemFit · 1–5
06 Where the study is

A small, careful pilot.
Then a real test.

The instrument is in its second cleaned version after a 5-person cognitive walkthrough. Next: a broader undergraduate sample, then a head-to-head between the interest-only model and the deeper model.

  1. Done · 2025 Draft v1 First instrument built from a literature review of interest, fit, burnout, and persistence research.
  2. Done · 2025 Cleaned v2.1 Items revised. Burnout separated from resilience. Interest history reframed. Three attention checks added.
  3. In progress · Now 5-person cognitive pilot Five undergraduates walk through the form aloud. We catch unclear, repetitive, or hard-to-answer items.
  4. Next Revise items Items flagged in cognitive interviews get rewritten or dropped. Pilot-only debrief questions removed.
  5. Then Larger collection Open the cleaned instrument to a broader undergraduate sample for statistical analysis.
  6. Final Compare models Test whether the deeper, multi-construct model predicts persistence better than interest alone.
07 Why It Matters

Two ways to predict
whether a student stays.

The whole point of the pilot is to set up a fair test: does adding behavioral, environmental, and psychological signals actually beat using interest alone?

Model 1 · Baseline

Interest only

Interest → Persistence?
  • One signal in
  • Mirrors what most online major quizzes do today
  • Easy to compute, easy to explain

Predicts only the surface. Misses the students who like the subject but can't sustain the environment.

Model 2 · Deeper fit

Interest + psychology + behavior + environment

Interest Env. fit Engagement Motivation Burnout Resilience Satisfaction Belonging → Persistence + Switch risk
  • Eleven validated constructs as inputs
  • Same person, same outcome — much richer picture
  • Designed to surface the students at quiet risk of switching
  • Validated against a single global-fit criterion item

More complete, not magical. The model can't make a student stay — it can only tell us which signals matter and where support might land.
